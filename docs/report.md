# Beyond the Shelf: Insights from a Power BI Sales Performance Analysis

**Original report by Christian Iyayi**  
**Source:** [Medium article](https://medium.com/@krisnice2002gmail.com/beyond-the-shelf-insights-from-a-power-bi-sales-performance-analysis-9e601d430cb1)

## Overview

How I explored revenue, profitability, product demand, customer satisfaction, and why reliable insights depend on asking better questions.

### Where should a snack business focus its attention on growing sales?

That question guided my Snacks Sales Performance Dashboard. Using Power BI, I explored how products, brands, and regions drive revenue, and how sales volume and customer satisfaction can shape business priorities.

Across 200 records, the dataset captures 56,195 units sold, £277,652.03 in revenue, and £111,065.62 in recorded profit. The overall recorded profit margin is 40%, with an average selling price of 4.94 per unit.

The opportunity is to use these patterns to guide stock planning, regional development, and focused commercial tests.

Figures cover all months in the dataset. Monetary amounts are expressed in the currency units used in the source data.

## How I built the analysis

I imported the Excel dataset into Power BI, used Power Query to prepare data types and month ordering, and developed DAX measures for revenue, recorded profit, units sold, average selling price, and margin.

The dashboard brings together eight categories, ten recorded brands, and five regions. Interactive controls allow users to explore individual months and switch between revenue, profit, volume, and margin.

This makes it possible to examine performance from several angles before choosing an action.

## 1. Balance revenue leaders with volume drivers

Nuts generated the highest category of revenue at £51,084.92, contributing 18.4% of total revenue from 7,183 units.

Chocolate followed with £44,566.56, representing 16.1%, while Fruit Snacks generated £42,832.89, or 15.4%. However, Fruit Snacks led sales volume with 8,448 units, ahead of Chocolate’s 7,991 units.

Together, these three categories contributed approximately half of total revenue.

The remaining categories also offer opportunities:

- Crackers: £29,902.60 in revenue from 7,828 units.
- Cookies: £29,878.95 from 5,304 units.
- Pretzels: £27,596.58 from 6,791 units.
- Chips: £27,094.46 from 5,824 units.
- Candy: £24,695.07 from 6,826 units.

### Business opportunity

Give revenue contribution and customer demand equal attention. Prioritise availability for leading categories, monitor replenishment needs, and test complementary product offers. Measure whether each test increases revenue and profit without creating excess stock.

## 2. Use regional performance to direct growth efforts

West led regional sales with £68,284.11 in revenue, representing 24.6% of the total, and 13,232 units sold.

This performance suggests that customer demand is particularly strong in the West, making it a valuable benchmark for understanding successful sales patterns and market conditions.

### Business insight

Regions that consistently outperform others can provide valuable lessons for growth. By examining the product mix, customer behaviour, and promotional strategies in high-performing areas, businesses can identify practices worth replicating in lower-performing regions. At the same time, investing in underperforming markets through targeted campaigns and localized product offerings may help unlock additional revenue opportunities.

## Key takeaway

This analysis demonstrates that effective business intelligence is not solely about reporting figures. Meaningful insights emerge when data is used to answer focused business questions. By combining revenue, profit, sales volume, and customer demand into a single analytical view, the dashboard helps reveal where growth opportunities exist and where business resources can be allocated most effectively.
